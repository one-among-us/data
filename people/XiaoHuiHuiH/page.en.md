## Introduction

XiaoHuiHui was an independent content creator, a Bilibili uploader, a trans girl, and a figure who attracted no small amount of controversy.

Starting in 2024, XiaoHuiHui gradually came out publicly as a trans girl and began hormone replacement therapy. That change in identity became a major turning point in her life. She began to relearn her own body, gender, and sense of self, and to ask herself: as the person she once knew drifts further away, how does one learn to live all over again?

She once wrote about her transformation under names like “Bro Ma” and “XiaoHuiHui,” placing her past and present selves within the same narrative. She never described her transition as a simple, bright path. Instead, she recorded the hesitation, the nostalgia, the struggle, and the slow work of rebuilding. She knew she had lost something familiar, yet she was also trying to embrace the “she” who could finally be fully recognized as her true self.

After moving to Canada, her creative work gradually shifted from the early internet meme culture toward more personal and grounded expression. She cared about the situation of trans people, and also about family control, the struggles of adolescents, accessibility, and other social issues. She often started from the “bad news” around her, asking how invisible pressures push people, step by step, toward hardship.

At the same time, her real life was far from calm. From 2025 into early 2026, she went through changes in close relationships, conflicts with roommates, and other complicated interpersonal disputes. The internet and reality became tangled together, leaving this later phase of her life filled with strain and exhaustion. She was not without fault, nor was she always clear-eyed and unwavering. She was simply someone trying to find her place.

In January 2026, she helped lead the production of the trans documentary *The “Fashion” We Consume Is the Life She Carries — A Trans Documentary*[^1], attempting to discuss how trans people are subjected to the public gaze, and how a specific person can keep her truth and dignity beyond labels. She had originally wanted to title the main film *Our Living Is the Greatest Resistance to Malice*, explaining that it was meant to encourage people in difficult circumstances to keep living.

In the final days of her life, she still kept in touch with friends. She also tried to reorganize her life, thank those who had helped her, and look for reasons to keep going.

On February 13, 2026, XiaoHuiHui passed away in Toronto. She was only 21.

Her life cannot be summed up by any single identity, any single controversy, or the last period of her life. After coming out as trans, she knew confusion and she knew courage; she knew loss and she knew what it was to regain something. While she watched herself, she never stopped watching others.

She tried to answer “Who am I?” She also tried to tell people in difficult circumstances:
> Our living is the greatest resistance to malice.

## ServalCandle’s Memory

The first time I met HuiHui was in March 2024.

Back then, the snow in Toronto had not fully melted. I had just been through one of the worst things in my life, felt like my life was a joke, and had just survived a suicide attempt.

That day was at the Eaton Centre. She came bouncing over, full of energy, and even photobombed a live news broadcast, acting cute and flashing a peace sign behind the host.

“She’s so cute. I really want to be her friend.”
That was the first thought that popped into my head when I saw her in person.

I’m not exaggerating when I say that in that moment, I suddenly didn’t want to die as much anymore.

Later, we really did become friends.

After that, every tweet I posted, I hoped she would see it. I always looked forward to her likes and replies.

I rarely offer to take photos for other people. Apart from my family and my boyfriend, there was only her.

She always thought I took good photos of her. Some she posted to Twitter, and some she used as her WeChat avatar.

But there was one thing I could never understand.

She was already trapped in her own abyss, yet she kept trying to rescue MTFs back in China.

She cared about other people. She cared about what vulnerable groups were going through, but it seemed like she never really took care of herself.

She once came to Waterloo and stayed at my place. When she left, she didn’t even have enough money to get home. Because almost all the money she had, she had given to MTFs suffering domestic abuse.

I felt helpless about it.

But maybe that’s just who she was. She simply couldn’t bear to see other people’s pain. She was so kind that she almost forgot herself.

In the end, I couldn’t help but give her some money.

I made a point of telling her that I was helping her not because she was also MTF, but because she was my best friend.

I hoped she would pay more attention to herself.

I hoped she could save herself too.

But she kept focusing on other people’s pain, leaving herself behind, bit by bit.

Later, I started replying to her on Twitter with more and more sarcasm, and we argued more and more. The way she addressed me also changed from “bestie” at the beginning to the half-joking “frenemy bestie” later on.

Looking back now, those arguments seem so far away.

I once said to her privately:
“You’re not gonna die on me or something, are you? ’Cause then I’d be stuck talking to your avatar every day.”

But she really left.

And I really did start talking to her avatar.

Even now, I still can’t fully accept that HuiHui is gone.

Someone told me she often mentioned me when she was alive.

Now it’s my turn to often mention her.

I’ve thought of countless possibilities.

What if I had been in Toronto when it happened?

What if I had cared a little more at the time?

What if I had done more than just recommend a lawyer?

Maybe the ending would have been different.

Recently, I learned about a theory called “Multiverse Immortality.”

So I can’t help but think: maybe in some universe, she really did wake up in the hospital.

And then everything went back to normal.

She was still the person who would bounce into the frame and act cute behind a news host.

Her documentary would premiere as planned. That was her biggest dream while she was alive.

We would still argue. We would still trade snark with each other. We would still call each other “frenemy bestie.”

And maybe the me in that world would think all of it was just ordinary.

I hope the me in that world realizes how lucky she is.
