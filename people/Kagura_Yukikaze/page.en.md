<h3 align="center">Farewell, Yukikaze</h3>

Today, the person we are saying goodbye to is named Yukikaze.

When she left this world, she was less than a month away from her seventeenth birthday, but she couldn’t wait any longer.

I often think that people like us are as if born without a passport, and can’t even get the qualifications to apply for one, carrying only a temporary residence permit. Yukikaze just couldn’t keep applying anymore; she grew tired of running around.

But what kind of person was Yukikaze? Like everyone here, she had a heart of gold. She was kind, she spread love and warmth, and she tried with all her might to love this world, even though this world never truly loved her in return.

Today, I found her lover and learned all of this:

On February 22nd of this year, they met each other. They lived on the same street, just a few hundred steps apart. They attended the same middle school, just two grades apart.

At 3 PM on March 1st, they met offline for the first time. Yukikaze gave her a fishcake keychain[^1] and bought her a pink Monster Energy drink. When it was time to leave the arcade, Yukikaze rode her electric scooter to take her home. Downstairs at her apartment, Yukikaze hugged her for the first time. She said that warmth... it was so incredibly warm.

Every weekend after that, they would appear at the arcade near their homes. From the two of them huddling together chatting, to holding hands, hugging, and pressing their cheeks together.

In April, they got matching glasses frames. On May 4th, they took the subway together to Guangzhou for the first time, strolling through Anime Star City and going to the Firefly Anime Convention. On her birthday, May 19th, she made only one wish for the first time—to be with her older sister forever, and never be separated.

During the Dragon Boat Festival in June, Yukikaze said she was going back to Anhui. She cried, and Yukikaze comforted her. But Yukikaze was also sick—even though she was simultaneously a crisis intervention volunteer. Sleeping at midnight and waking up at 6 AM, her family had smashed her phone and computer, refusing to let her go to the hospital, so she could only buy raw lithium carbonate to take herself. Throughout the entire semester, Yukikaze carried the emotional burdens of others, her own illness, and the knives at home.

She carried far too many things she shouldn’t have had to. Yet she never stopped loving others, never stopped reaching out to pull someone up.

After the high school entrance exams, Yukikaze decided to accompany her until July 15th. They went to the Apple Store, Starbucks, IKEA, and wandered all over Foshan. Once, in a hospital corridor, Yukikaze said her wish was to get her diagnosis certificate after coming of age, and save money for bottom surgery. That day, she confessed her feelings to Yukikaze. She said Yukikaze had already surpassed her biological older sister.

July 14th, the last day. They went to the biggest square, walking hand in hand, kissing and hugging in empty corners, sharing the same bowl of tong sui (sweet dessert soup).

> On the way back, sitting on the backseat of Yukikaze’s scooter, she said:
>
> “Let’s be together.”
>
> Yukikaze nodded.
> 
> “We are biological sisters, and also each other’s girlfriends.”

They laughed so loudly that day. Downstairs at her apartment, Yukikaze handed her a box of gifts—anime badges, skincare products, and two blank CDs. Then Yukikaze rode away. She watched that fading silhouette, not knowing it would be the last time she ever saw her.

Yukikaze said she would wait for her to return, but ultimately broke her promise.

I think of the letters Qiu Miaojin wrote in her final days in Paris, every word saying: I tried, but this world didn’t give me a place to stand.

Yukikaze was the same. It wasn’t that she didn’t love her; she loved her too much, loved her until she couldn’t hold on anymore.

Yukikaze’s lover said:
“The younger sister has always loved her older sister. From the first meeting to her departure, from being together to being separated, she loved her every single moment. No matter if the older sister is a biological sister or something else in the younger sister’s heart, she is the person the younger sister cherishes the most in this life. At least right now, the older sister is the most precious, the most valued person. She will always be the most important person. The younger sister will never forget her older sister. Those memories will always be there; they will always be beautiful memories.”

I want to say to Yukikaze: Sixteen years, you owe no one. You left, and I do not blame you. But the next time you come, remember to find a world that has gotten better, good enough that you can stay.

The pastor said: Her identity is not a sin, her love is not a mistake. The Lord knows her as she truly is.

The priest said: She has returned to the light, and there shall be no more tears.

Yukikaze is gone. But we are still here. We live, not to atone for sins, but to prove—this world didn’t deserve her death, but we deserve to live.

We must see seventeen, eighteen, twenty... for her. We must wear pretty clothes for her, eat sweet things for her, and be called by our right names for her. Live for her, until no more of our kind have to die just for being themselves.

> Yukikaze, Maomao, Xiao Chen, Susu, Tangtang.
> 
> They are not individual accidents. For a child to be themselves, they have to climb over the walls of family, school, society, law, and healthcare—every single wall. Some make it over, and some remain forever at the foot of the wall. We are not mourning death; we are mourning that this world has not yet learned to be gentle.
> 
> On behalf of Yukikaze’s lover, and all those who could not come, I thank you. Thank you for not turning away, thank you for speaking her name. She has not disappeared; she lives on in your words.

Finally, I want to say to Yukikaze’s lover: The one who left today is not just Yukikaze, but too many of our kind like her. Every one of us who is alive must help each other, support each other, and survive together. Living is victory. We must live well for her, and live well for all of our kind who have passed away. Us being together is the longest echo she leaves behind in this world.

We are creating a tomorrow, we are living for tomorrow, we are constantly fighting, we will always move forward, and our tomorrow will be bathed in sunlight, full of light!

Today is Sunday, time rushes by, and seven days have also ushered in the tide of life chasing into the distance. I want to gently stroke the sea, paper planes heading far away. Please, let this breeze not stop, follow the sea and the horizon, remember to look back, there is a child looking out at the pier, keeping an appointment. Bring back the light birds, smell the breath of spring, circle the streets, and listen to the noise of the city.

Gently stroke the sea... gently stroke the sea... a child is looking out at the pier...

Yukikaze. You are free. You are home. We love you. We will not forget.

Dedicated to Yukikaze (August 12, 2009 - July 17, 2026)

Comrade Xiaozhi, a transgender person, written at 1:00 AM on July 18, 2026.

[^1]: Fishcake: Here it refers to a keychain featuring the “Narutomaki” (🍥) design. Because its pattern resembles the appearance of “Progynova”, an estrogen medication commonly used by transgender women, it is often used by the transgender community in mainland China as a subtle symbol for identity and mutual recognition.
