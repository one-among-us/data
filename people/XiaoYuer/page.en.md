<h3 align="center">I: In Memoriam</h3>

<p style="text-align: center;">Maple</p>

## Introduction

Xiaoyu’er was a warm and kind-hearted child. She loved almost every kind of transport, loved chemistry and anything related to science, engineering and technology, and always carried in her heart a dream of deep space and a longing for science.

She once told me that chemical reactions never lie, and that the light and heat within them are the most beautiful things in the world.

> When working with hazardous chemicals, always protect yourself properly, meow~

She was very fond of locomotive SS8-0166, and because of my own fondness for it, she once travelled from Nanjing to the Jinhua–Wenzhou Railway to see the locomotives in the line’s special livery[^1]. From 2024 onwards, she also collected a great many “red tickets”[^2].

## Beginnings

I first met her in 2023. We were both in middle school then. We came to know each other because we shared the same dream, and soon became friends who told each other everything.

At the time, I had just started planning a project involving weather balloons and sounding rockets. I asked in a group chat whether anyone could design PLCs, and she added me.

> “I can design PLCs and write flight-control code.”
>
> “Wow, could you teach me?”
>
> “Designing circuit boards is easy. I’ll teach you when I have time, meow~”

That day was an early-autumn evening in 2023. Meeting her was a lovely thing. That night, a group of middle schoolers from all over the country, carrying passion and dreams, came together.

At that moment, the Earth was a star in the sky, and Nanjing was a city on the ground. The night was as calm as any other, and yet our fates had been woven together.

> We all said there would be time later. In the end, we all went our separate ways.
>
> — 2026-10-07 22:30:40, Xiaoyu’er

## A Wish Upon the Stars

At the start of 2024, at the beginning of the new year, Xiaoyu’er was in Grade 8 and I was in Grade 9. On New Year’s Eve we were discussing the weather-balloon plan. Late that night, we began talking about each other’s hobbies, families, and lives.

That was when she told me she was MTX, and I told her I was MTF. It was also then that I learned she had already taken a leave from school because of her mental health.

We talked happily that night, and for the first time we stepped into each other’s inner worlds. After that night, she started calling me “big sister”, and I came to see her as a very capable teacher and a very capable little sister.

By April 2024, we had drafted a rough schedule. But because of Xiaoyu’er’s mental health and my own, we never ended up carrying it out.

> “Sorry, everyone. It looks like we’ll only be able to carry out our plan in 2025 or 2026.”
>
> “It’s all right. Take it slow. Pat pat.”

And then, once again, a group of children were separated by reality.

Only this time, after we parted, we never came together again. Everyone still carried curiosity about the stars and dreams of the sky, but dreams are ultimately just dreams. No matter how grand a dream is, it is not enough to change reality.

## Shattered Dream

The dream had stopped in its tracks, but life went on. That was the middle of 2024. In our daily lives we had become friends who told each other everything, always ready to comfort each other when the other was hurting.
It seemed as though everything was heading in a good direction. But then members of the team began to stop working because of public opinion and social pressure, and the group chat went cold…

> “Sorry. Let’s postpone it indefinitely…”
>
> “We won’t have time next year.”
>
> “I know… Sorry, everyone…”

After that day, the group fell completely silent. Xiaoyu’er came to me and cried for a long, long time. She was clearly in so much pain, yet she told me it was all right, that there would be time later—comforting me, and comforting herself too.
After that day, we buried the selves who had once been so full of passion…

> And then, there was a child who, before the old wounds had healed, was pushed without mercy into this cruel adult world.

After that, she turned her life back toward public transport and began making frequent railfanning trips, photographing the locomotives she loved. She made chemistry and railways into substitutes for her dream, and I believe she must have fallen back in love with those old hobbies along the way.

## Trauma and Pain

By 2025, Xiaoyu’er’s emotional state had already begun to falter. She had depression to begin with, and in 2024 she came into contact with the trans community. That environment made her already fragile mental state even worse.

At that time, it was her second year on leave from school. At sixteen she should have been in high school, but because of a year’s leave she was still in Grade 8, and her mental illness had begun to cause massive memory loss.

Eventually she lost her memories of 2024 and 2025, and she forgot me too. By the time she remembered again, it was already September 2026.

By then she had been diagnosed with BD (bipolar disorder), and she had begun to feel constantly lost and afraid. She said the past two years had been terrible and frightening.

> “@Maple I think I only remembered you a couple of months ago. When I thought of you, all I remembered was that we were very close, that we once had a shared goal of launching rockets and balloons, and that there was a group.”
> “I feel like there’s a huge chunk of my memory of you missing.”
>
> “Maple: Are you okay?”
>
> “Maybe I’m just very sick. Maybe my parents pushed me until I had nowhere to vent.”

That day was the first time in six months that we had talked so happily again. We talked about the joys and sorrows of the past, and about how our friends were doing.

In the end, we talked about the future. She said she would go and see a doctor in a few days, try to get better, and then take the high-school entrance exam next year, and bring her life back on track.

## An Early Death

On 11 October 2026, she left this world.

On 21 September 2026 and 5 October 2026 she had both attempted suicide, but the third time, fate did not favour her…

The police and her friends classified her death as suicide, but it was not suicide. It was a tragedy brought about by domestic violence, and her parents could have prevented it many times over.

But they made mistake after mistake. It began with them making her stop taking her psychiatric medication, then telling her that it was somatization, that it was dissociation, and finally planning to drag her off to see a Chinese Medicine doctor. It was under this relentless pressure that they pushed her to the edge.

> My way out kept getting narrower. They said my body mattered, and in their eyes everything I did was harming my body. But was my body really all right? I overdose, and they say it harms my body—fine, I accept that. I cut my wrists, and they say it harms my body—fine, I accept that. I take hormones, and I accept that too. But even when I take my medication normally, they think I’m harming my body.  
> I take my medication and sleep, and they say I’m taking too much, that I’m doubling up. If I could sleep well, would I need to take so much? My hair falls out a lot, and they blame the medication. I have no energy during the day, and they blame the medication. And after all that blaming, they want to drag me off to see a Chinese Medicine doctor and make me drink Chinese herbal decoction.

I blame her parents, and in truth I blame myself as well. I blame my slowness, my uselessness. I had many chances to save her, and I failed to take them…

## Shattered Stars | Silence

In the end, she still broke her promise. We had agreed that she would definitely come and see me at least once in this lifetime…
Once, a little sister told me that she admired me and hoped to become like me. At the time I was too insecure to take it seriously. Only after losing her did I realize she had meant it.
And then came self-reproach and grief. I had thought I would no longer be pained by anyone’s departure, but when I confirmed she was gone, on a flight overseas, my heart twisted in pain.

Suddenly I realized an old friend was gone forever, and tears soaked through my clothes.

> “@Maple You’re someone I admire too.”  
> “@Maple I’ll definitely meet you at least once in this lifetime, my big sister.”  
> “I feel like I won’t live much longer. My mental state this year has been terrible. I’m going into hospital in a couple of days—I hope it cures me. But I beg them not to give me MECT…”

In the end, in the very end, the story is still just a story.

In this world, there was never a Little Fish who was always optimistic—only a child who hoped the future would be better.

She left her beloved collection behind in a summer that will never end.

Fate turned a corner in the river of time. It left behind a past full of regret and a final parting. If there is a next life, please be happy.

Goodnight, Little Fish.

<h3 align="center">II: Farewell</h3>

<p style="text-align: center;">LinWaterMoon</p>

## Introduction

Xiaoyu’er was a bus and train enthusiast from Nanjing who, like many transgender people, simply wished to make herself look beautiful.

> Xiaoyu’er—it seems quite a few people go by this handle, too.

## Sudden Tragic News

> *A message, a phone call, and a silent, motionless silence. Only the songs playing in the earphones kept echoing on and on.*

On October 11, 2026, LinWaterMoon woke up as usual—a little early that day, waking at 7:30 AM. He picked up his phone to check his messages as usual, and saw a message Xiaoyu’er had sent at 2:30 in the morning: “Goodnight. Only you won’t save me,” along with several photos showing chemical reagent bottles.

At noon that day, LinWaterMoon received a call from a police officer in Nanjing… From that moment on, the music in his earphones never stopped for the rest of the day…

> “But you clearly promised you were coming to visit me over winter break...”

In her suicide note, Xiaoyu’er wrote that for about four years she had longed to have attractive features, longed to become a cute, beloved girl, and even wanted to become a femboy or live as a trans girl. But in reality, she felt extreme disgust and inferiority toward her facial features, voice, figure, and body hair—even viewing herself as an “aqua-themed femboy exam-grub,”[^3] utterly unworthy of being a trans girl. She thought she was very ugly; every time she saw a cute femboy or a pretty girl online, an intense yearning would well up inside her. She had hoped to change her situation by wearing nice clothes and cosplaying anime characters, but because she lacked the confidence and courage to express herself, and was terrified of others noticing the changes or facing her family’s interference and opposition, she ultimately gave up trying. She once dreamed that her HRT regimen was discovered, and dreamed her mother said to her: “If you keep going with this, I’ll kill you!” Yet she insisted on continuing, and so in the dream, she was killed. It might sound somewhat ridiculous—but she really did have such a dream.

Her suicide was not out of hope for a better reincarnation in the next life; she simply wanted to end everything.

> If you cannot choose the process, and it is not a process you like, it is better to end everything early.
> 
> Written like a pile of shit—I know I write terribly, but this is all I can manage.
> 
> Goodnight, world.

## Past Recordings and Remaining Memories

A week passed. LinWaterMoon received a private message—a screenshot from a video, in which the sender had drawn a box around one person: her hair neither long nor short, a pair of black-framed glasses, a black shirt, and a phone in her hand, filming as well.

“Is this Xiaoyu’er?” the person asked, and then sent the video.

The footage had been taken at a birthday gathering in Shanghai of someone like her—someone of the same kind. In the frame she was laughing and talking—shaking a glow sign and then she too took out her phone and filmed along with them. Sitting around Xiaoyu’er were others like her, transgender women of the same kind. It was one of the very few moments when she could take off her disguise and feel a sense of belonging and happiness.

> When she met fellow trans girls like her, it must have been her happiest, most blissful moment.

“It’s her… That’s definitely her.” Looking at the familiar outfit in the video and the look of her phone, LinWaterMoon answered firmly. “When I travelled with her last year, she dressed just like that.”

On that trip last year, Xiaoyu’er had gone with LinWaterMoon to several cities and met many Twitter/X mutuals. When he first met Xiaoyu’er, LinWaterMoon had joked that she “was a handsome young fellow, about eighty or ninety percent like what a big shot holding a ‘a camera with a massive telephoto lens’ looked like in his younger days”—little knowing that such a joke had invisibly become a hidden sting that worsened her gender dysphoria.

In later private messages, Xiaoyu’er also unreservedly poured out to LinWaterMoon her dependence and her despair:

> “Don’t you call me Chen So-and-so!”  
> “I’m so tired. The trip with you last year feels like the last time I felt truly happy… I’ve never been this blissful.”  
> “I really want to be with you.”  
> “I’ve fallen for you.”  
> “I really want to come over to your place *w*”  
> “I really want to be happy with you… but I don’t think I’ll live that long…”  
> “I’m crying again… it’s become something I have to go through every night before sleep.”  
> “Let’s just leave it at that. End this topic.”  
> “I want to buy a computer—do you have any recommended specs or brands? I’m asking seriously.”

In the early hours of October 11, 2026, she finally decided—to leave this world and end it all.

> “Goodnight. Only you won’t save me.”

## Her Parents Came Looking

A few days later, LinWaterMoon met Xiaoyu’er’s parents—her mom and dad came together. “I’d better step outside for a moment,” her mom choked back tears as soon as she sat down. “Hearing this is still a bit too painful for me…”

“He [sic] must have left quite bravely—quite resolutely. He probably had no attachments left to this world… Over the past few years, he was taking leave from school and returning intermittently; he had very few friends—maybe because he was naturally quite insecure. He didn’t want to depend on the family or take our money; he wanted to earn a living through his own ability… He cried and shared some of what was in his heart with me. Before, he was afraid the family would object, so he didn’t dare say anything. He said he wanted to keep a cat, and that he also wanted to make himself pretty…” Xiaoyu’er’s father said calmly.

Xiaoyu’er once said that she and LinWaterMoon were very much alike—very similar in personality, yet somehow different as well.

> “I like LinWaterMoon the most,” Xiaoyu’er once said.

Just a few days before Xiaoyu’er left, she said she wanted to keep a cat. Her family agreed and promised to buy her one. They had planned to go on the 11th to buy the cat she had picked out and bring it home—but that cat would never meet the owner who belonged to it.

## A Hastily Written Ending

That day, the cat was still curled up in the pet shop, while Xiaoyu’er was forever frozen in that icy early morning. She took with her all the unutterable struggles and self-loathing, leaving this world with nothing but that softly spoken “Goodnight,” and “Only you won’t save me.” The sun rose as usual, but it could no longer reach the shadow in Xiaoyu’er’s heart. And for those who cared about her, this quiet farewell remains a wound that will never heal.

> Before you left, you even asked if I wanted to make a pink-blue-white background for your avatar…

[^1]: Special livery: The DF4B diesel locomotives assigned to the Jinhua–Wenzhou Railway were nicknamed “white melons” (白兰瓜, báilánguā) by Chinese railfans because of their white body with blue stripes.

[^2]: Red Tickets: A colloquial term among Chinese railway enthusiasts for the old-style red paper train tickets, as opposed to the later blue magnetic tickets. Xiaoyu’er collected them as a railway enthusiast.

[^3]: Aqua-Themed Femboy Exam-Grub: A self-deprecating compound term coined by Xiaoyu’er. “Aqua-themed” refers to a light-blue/pastel-aqua fashion aesthetic common in subculture fashion; “femboy” refers to an effeminate or cute cross-dressing boy/trans-feminine individual; “exam-grub” is a harsh China mainland internet slur for someone who does nothing but grind practice exams without real life or taste. Combined, it conveys a self-loathing stereotype within the community: a test-grinding femboy who, even dressed in pastel aqua outfits, is mocked for having poor aesthetic taste and being unable to shake off the drab, soul-crushing aura of an exam drill machine. Xiaoyu’er used it to put herself down—feeling that no matter how much she longed to be a pretty trans girl, she was ultimately just a “exam grub” beyond saving, even in nice clothes.
